## 0. How to read this

Two conventions run through the whole document:

- **`[[INPUT: ...]]`** — a cell only Nadav, Avishag or Ken can fill. It is empty
  because the figure has not been stated anywhere I can read, not because it is
  unimportant. Several of them are load-bearing; the ones that decide the year
  are called out in §6.
- **`[[NADAV: ...]]`** — a decision that has not been made yet, not a number.

Every filled figure traces to a source: a mail thread, the GPU Compute Deal
Tracker, or the Cohort 3 response sheet. Nothing here is estimated, benchmarked
against comparables, or inferred. Where two sources disagree, both are shown and
the disagreement is listed as an open item rather than averaged away.

Targets marked **(stated)** were set by Nadav. Targets marked **(proposed)** are
mine, derived from a stated baseline, and are up for argument.

1. **Apex is funded past the cliff.** The $2M closes by 15.10.2026 and the year

## 4. The bridge — talent + hardware

Nadav's own framing:

> הכי קל להביא לזה off taker (וכבר יש) אבל משמעותי בהרבה עבור יזמים צעירים.
> טאלנט + hardware.

*The easy part is finding an offtaker — and one already exists. The far more
meaningful thing is what it does for young founders. Talent + hardware.*

**The thesis.** Apex and VelocityX have 40 founders and 116 vetted technical
people, most from elite tracks. Tinu has compute those founders cannot otherwise
buy at any sane price. Neither is unusual alone. Together they are: a fund whose
portfolio gets compute nobody else can offer, and a compute business whose first
20–40 tenants arrive pre-vetted and warm.

**The mechanism.** The 20–40 early-stage startups Tinu is building for and the
Apex/VelocityX founder base are, deliberately, the same list. The Israeli 0.5 MW
is the natural first home for them.

**Shared KRs** — these belong to neither entity alone:

| KR | Baseline | Target |
|---|---|---|
| KR9.1 Apex/VX companies on Tinu compute | 0 | **≥ 10 by Aug 2027** (proposed) |
| KR9.2 Tinu tenants sourced from the Apex base | 0 | **≥ 40% of tenants** (proposed) |
| KR9.3 A stated compute offer for the community | none | **published terms by end Q1** (proposed) |

**KR9.3 first.** Right now there is no artefact a founder can be pointed at. A
one-page offer — what capacity, what price, what commitment — converts the
thesis into something Avishag can send.

**Stated plainly: the conflict.** Apex is a nonprofit. VelocityX invests in
founders. Tinu sells those founders compute. The structure is a genuine
advantage and also a genuine question a funder or an LP will ask, probably in
the middle of the $2M raise. Better to have the answer written before 15.10 than
composed in the room. `[[NADAV: what is the answer — pricing at cost for
portfolio? arm's length? disclosed to whom?]]`
