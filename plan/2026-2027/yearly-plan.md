# APEX — yearly plan, September 2026 → August 2027

**Owner:** Nadav Delgo · **Reviewed with:** Avishag, weekly
**Scope:** APEX talent, alumni and network, with the VelocityX bridge

> אני לא רוצה שתהיה "האדם שעושה שיחות עם הבוגרים". אני רוצה שתגיע למצב שבו אתה
> יודע יותר ממני על מה שקורה אצל האנשים של APEX. אתה ה־Human Node של APEX.

That sentence is the whole job. This plan is the operating system for it: who
gets called on which day, where what they need is written down, and how a
conversation turns into something that actually helps them.

Everything here derives from the role definition. Section references (§) point
at it. Where the plan invents a mechanism rather than restating a requirement,
it says so.

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | What |
|---|---|
| **Sunday** | Plan the week. Send the Monday briefing (§16) before the meeting, never in it. Two calls. |
| **Monday** | Weekly People Review with Avishag — 60 min, plus up to 30 for a deep dive only when genuinely needed (§15). One call. |
| **Tue–Wed** | Calls. Follow-ups from Monday's decisions. |
| **Thursday** | Weekly report (§17), the three lines (§21), roster hygiene. No calls — this is deliberate slack for the week's slippage. |

Between meetings the work is email and asynchronous. WhatsApp is for short or
genuinely urgent things; phone when it truly cannot wait. **90 minutes a week
with Avishag is the default ceiling**, and if something was sent once it does
not get re-sent through another channel (§15).

**Preference order for a conversation:** coffee, then Zoom, then phone (§5).
The schedule names the person and the purpose; the channel is a judgement call.

## Q1 · September–November 2026 — Know them

Built backwards from §24. In ninety days these must be answerable without
asking anyone:

- [ ] **Who are our 20 strongest alumni?** — 27 tier-A alumni each called once
      by 30 Nov. The rotation already schedules this.
- [ ] **What is each of them doing now?** — every tier-A row carries
      `current_goal`, `needs`, `can_help`.
- [ ] **Who is on the way to founding?** — the founding-track question is the
      standing purpose on every tier-A call.
- [ ] **Who already helps others?** — `tie_strength` 4–5 identified and named.
- [ ] **Who are our people in SF?** — SF phases 1–2 complete (map, then meet).
- [ ] **Do we have the first 20 Office Hours contributors?** — 8 by end of Q1.
- [ ] **Which relationships have moved from Avishag to Nadav?** — tracked
      weekly as a KPI from week one, so the trend line exists by Q4.
- [ ] **The 16 unknowns resolved** — role, company, current goal on file.

**Also in Q1**
- Coffee Club #1 (early Oct) and #2 (early Dec) — one alum per cohort (§10).
- Office Hours research **before** launching anything (§12): how other networks
  run this, and above all how they prevent abuse, overload and burnout. Bring a
  recommendation, not a launch.
- Contacts export merged, so the schedule shows numbers rather than LinkedIn.
- Baseline the Avishag-dependency number (§18). A baseline that looks bad is
  fine; a missing one makes the whole year unmeasurable.

**The Q1 risk.** Ninety days of data-gathering can produce a beautiful database
and no relationships. The guard is that every call ends with a next action and
at least one thing APEX can actually do for that person — §3's "השבחה", not a
check-in.

## Q2 · December–February — Harness them

The turn from receiving to giving (§4). APEX helps alumni a lot, but it is not
a service for alumni.

- **Office Hours to 20 contributors**, with the anti-abuse model agreed:
  curated not open, a specific ask rather than "I'd love to pick your brain",
  limited frequency, reciprocity expected over time (§12).
- **First contributor cohort active** — target 12 alumni who have helped
  another alum at least once.
- **SF phase 3** — create value there and keep relationships warm when we need
  nothing from them. This is the phase that gets skipped and the one that
  decides whether the network is real.
- **Coffee Clubs #3 and #4**, each producing follow-up actions Nadav owns.
- **Tier B swings through its second pass**, now asking what they can give
  rather than what they need.

**Measured by:** alumni who helped another alum (§17), and the funnel (§19)
starting to move: Alumni → Engaged → Helped → Contributors → Nodes.

## Q3 · March–May — Compound

The point at which the network stops routing through one person.

- **Alumni-to-alumni introductions exceed Nadav-brokered ones.** This is the
  single clearest sign the thing is working.
- **SF phase 4** — extend beyond Avishag's circle. An APEX alum landing in SF
  walks into an existing network, not a strange city (§11).
- **A founder-density dinner** — eight potential founders, not another broad
  event. §21's own worked example, and the right shape for what Q1–Q2 will
  have surfaced.
- **Connection quality reviewed** (§20): of the introductions made, how many
  went to a second conversation, a collaboration, a job, a design partnership,
  and how many went nowhere. This is how we learn which matching works, and it
  needs a full year's tail to be worth reading.
- **Coffee Club #5.**

## Standing projects

### Alumni Roll-Up (§9)
A live picture of every alum, not a list of names. Done when every row carries
role, company, current goal and a next action. **Backlog at start: 16 with no
verified role.** Q1 clears those; the rotation keeps the rest current.

### Coffee Club (§10) — every two months
Four alumni, one per cohort, plus Avishag and Nadav. Two purposes: **listen
through them** — what is happening with alumni, what they need, what APEX does
well and what we cannot see — and **harness them**, starting to make each one
active in the network. Ask directly: how can you help others at APEX?

Nadav owns making it happen and syncing the follow-ups afterwards. Nobody
repeats within the year. It is not called Coffee Club out loud.

### SF connection (§11)
Turn the Valley network from something that depends on Avishag into APEX
infrastructure.

1. **Map the existing circle** (Q1)
2. **Meet the people who matter in it** (Q1)
3. **Create value; stay warm when we need nothing** (Q2)
4. **Extend beyond Avishag's circle** (Q3–Q4)

SF-bound alumni go into the SF group. Every alum who mentions a move gets
`location` updated the same day — that is what makes this searchable later.

### Office Hours Network (§12)
Twenty quality people willing to give time. Per person: expertise, role and
company, location, what they help with, at which stage, how often, preferred
format, whether an intro is needed, and their boundaries.

Examples of expertise: AI Research, Fundraising, GTM, Product, Compute,
Hiring, US Expansion, Enterprise, Defense, Founder Coaching, Legal.

**Research the model before launching it.** Check access, matching, frequency,
quality control, contributor fatigue, confidentiality and follow-up. The time
of people who help us is a scarce resource and protecting it is part of the
design, not an afterthought.

## VelocityX — the bridge, not a second plan

APEX and VelocityX are run together and share staff. This plan does not
duplicate a fund operating model; it wires the one link that matters.

**An alum who starts a company, joins a founding team, or begins raising is
simultaneously:**
- a §14 moment — a founder worth pulling close, exactly what Avishag asked to
  hear about;
- VelocityX deal flow — propose the Affinity entry.

`/call-log` flags that transition automatically. Reviewed monthly inside the
existing Weekly People Review — no new meeting. The roster tracks the person;
Affinity tracks the company.

With 27 tier-A alumni, several already founding, this is the highest-value
thing the two organisations share.

## What gets reported

### Monday, before the review (§16)
Written and sent ahead, so the hour is spent on people, opportunities,
judgement and decisions — never on transferring information.

- **Top People** — up to 5. `Name | Cohort | what changed | what they need | who owns it`
- **Opportunities** — up to 3
- **Risks / Weak Signals** — up to 3
- **Avishag Needed** — up to 3, each with: who, why now, why her specifically,
  what exactly, and how long it will take
- **This Week** — the 3–5 calls and what I want to learn in each

### Thursday (§17, §21)

| KPI | Week | Month |
|---|---|---|
| Meaningful alumni conversations | 4 | 15 |
| Alumni with current info in the system | +4 | 72% |
| Quality connections made | 3 | 11 |
| Connections that led to an outcome | 1 | 5 |
| Alumni who helped another alum | 2 | 7 |
| Office Hours contributors | +1 | 12/20 |
| New SF relationships | 1 | 6 |
| Relationships moved from Avishag to Nadav | 2 | 9 |
| Things that genuinely required Avishag | 2 | — |

Not every number rises every week. We are looking for trend and quality.

Plus the three that matter most:
- **Dependency (§18)** — "87% handled independently | 3 raised for decision |
  1 connection needed Avishag". 100% is not the goal.
- **The funnel (§19)** — Alumni → Engaged → Helped → Contributors → Nodes. Far
  more meaningful than "I did 17 calls".
- **Connection quality (§20)** — not how many introductions, but what came of
  them.

### And three lines (§21)
- **מה למדתי** — what did I learn about our people this week?
- **מה אני ממליץ** — what should APEX do about it?
- **מה אני צריך מאבישג** — max three. Zero is better.

## The rules this plan runs on

**Do (§23):** know people deeply · filter · spot talent early · know what each
person needs *now* · create value before asking · connect only when there is a
real reason · follow through · document · build your own relationships · turn
alumni into contributors and then nodes · bring Avishag only where her
involvement actually changes the outcome.

**Don't (§22):** send everyone to Avishag · use her as a CRM · ask her for
follow-up you can do · hand her a relationship just because she created it ·
make introductions without a reason · measure success in coffees · collect
contacts without relationships · give endless service without reciprocity ·
wait to be told the next action · send updates on WhatsApp all day · bring a
problem without a recommendation · use the shared hour to read out the week.

**The introduction gate (§3).** Before connecting two people, answer: *why do
these two need to know each other now?* No good answer, no introduction. The
goal is long-term relationships that produce value, not a count of intros. And
close the loop with the alum — even when Avishag does not reply, Delgo is the
one who makes sure the circle closes.

**Reciprocity (§4).** We want to help alumni a great deal. APEX is not a
service for alumni. Success is not how much we helped, but how many of them
became part of the engine that helps others.

## How to run it day to day

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `plan/2026-2027/call-schedule.csv` | today's calls, with the purpose of each |
| `/call-log` | paste a call summary → roster updated, follow-up drafted with real offers |
| `/network-sync` | Avishag's mail + WhatsApp exports → roster updated |
| `/weekly-brief` | Monday briefing · Thursday report |

The roster lives at `config/network/roster.csv`. It is the source of truth for
people; `config/network/network.yaml` holds the rules. When something is routed
or matched wrongly, fix the config rather than working around it.

**Nothing is ever sent.** Every email is a draft. Calendar entries are
suggested, never created — creating one mails invitations to other people.
